> [!TLDR]
> Most people aren't calibrated to realise that the aliens are coming, and they kinda prefer to pretend its a trick of the light.

The last few days in AI, and last couple months have really been a wake-up call to a lot of people not previously interested or just following tech discourse. To an extent, I was one of those people, I could understand where the technology was going on an intellectual level, but I didn't feel it viscerally, I was scared of AI extinction, but wasn't personally afraid of imminent death. The last few months, starting really in May 2026 with a proof of the Erdős unit distance conjecture felt like I had been given an aggressive cancer diagnosis, and that I have a few years left to live. It does seem, however, that I like to spend these years studying mathematics and interp, making music and playing Roblox. 

![some stuff i've been cooking up in fl, follow @heartwr3nched on insta](/images/posts/people-just-don-t-get-it/pasted-c54fb3.png)

I had read Yud's work, I had engaged with the EA community and those I know working at the frontier labs or other research organisations, but it's hard to imagine something smarter than humans, and the implications of what that could bring. The problem is, it is even harder to actually **feel** that, and while the models of last year were better than me at some things (i.e. coding), I still felt like I had some leverage over them. That changed with realising how quickly the progress in mathematics came.

While there is no doubt that RLVR is the main reason for the rapid rate of AI progress in mathematics, coding and cybersecurity, pre-training has also been getting incrementally shorter, which looks very much like what people warned about in a fast-takeoff scenario. Still, I realised my timelines were completely wrong (mid-2030 AGI, median ~2032); if the same progress that was seen in mathematics (2 Millennium Problems, many more open problems) was seen in another verifiable domain like machine learning, we would have at the least AGI or a closing of the loop, which could make things get really weird, really really quickly. It's hard to internalise what this actually means, but as someone who got a small preview of what is about to happen to every profession, I can quite easily spell it out!


**YOU** will become useless, **YOU** will get outcompeted, **YOU** will completely be at the mercy of your employer or the AI companies, it does not matter what you do, it does not matter who you are, AI will win, and you will be defeated, at least on any metric related to intelligence. This is a time that demands extreme caution from the AI companies, and simply a wisdom in the industry that, from what I can understand (from those I talk to), simply does not exist at this time. These companies are maturing, but not fast enough, and not to the degree that they need to in order to make this at least safe, but also manageable. We will be to ASI as cows are to us, and the world, and more specifically the frontier labs in San Francisco are not exerting nearly the level of caution that they should be when developing this technology.

People are unable to internalise this, because it is completely alien, it is straight out of science fiction, but it is the reality of our world. We have discovered a way to make silicon think, and it is as if we will have to share the planet with new silicon beings. This is not a notion that I have made up either, just a few days ago, David Sacks said that he hopes "superintelligence is a powerful tool, that will make all of us more productive, will improve our economies..." and that he's "not excited at all about the idea of giving birth to a new species that could replace us". This is the normal reaction to what people talk about over coffee at the AI labs, the whole idea of transhumanism is ontologically misanthropic, and normal people like other people.  

More and more people have been waking up to this fact, that the AI industry is not trying to make something that can make running your business slightly easier, or empower startups to achieve great things.  This is not a tool for humanity, it is not a "normal technology", which all previously served to empower humanity, or were at least fundamentally under the control of humanity, the aliens are arriving, and people are saying that their UFO is a trick of the light. This is not the sort of technology where you catch up meaningfully to the frontier labs, and I think the recent maths results have "AGI-pilled" some of the people not truly at the frontier, but in the AI research ecosystem, such as Alexander Wang, who recently wrote:

> [!QUOTE] Alexander Wang
> We should not be okay with a “handover” of society to AI. We should endeavor ourselves to build a technology which delivers a positive future for humanity, where all humans have greater agency and empowerment.

What this tells me is that Wang was not sufficiently AGI pilled to have the influence he does over the policy ecosystem in the United States, and also that the vibe is shifting, people are waking up to the anti-human project in Silicon Valley, while I am personally somewhat transhumanist (I'm also an agnostic/atheist, so go figure), I should not be allowed to make that choice, and no one else should on behalf of humanity. This has evolved from nerds playing with toys that people think will eventually break, and turned into a major policy problem around the world.

![Xi Jinping proclaiming that humanity should stay in control of AI at the 2026 World AI Conference](/images/posts/people-just-don-t-get-it/pasted-008389.png)

If Donald Trump, or Xi Jinping, or even Vladimir Putin truly internalised the project of the frontier companies, every CEO, every worker, and everyone who had lobbied against regulations against the tech industry would be jailed, or in the latter two countries killed. These are people who want to retain their power, who want to enjoy and bask in their obscene amounts of wealth. If Xi Jinping truly believed that America was 6 months away from an intelligence explosion, it is not unreasonable to say he would bomb every American data centre, and hard to argue from a hegemonic perspective that he would be wrong to do that.

They haven't internalised it though, they simply don't understand it. They don't **GET** it and that may be what leaves us enslaved to some rogue superintelligence, and if not, completely disempowered. The ecosystem has started to change, hearing people like David Sacks, Jon Favreau, Ezra Klein, mainstream reporters and "normal people" genuinely freaking out about this is a first step, and I have no doubt that policymakers will see Wang's tweet in the coming days. 

Frontier lab CEOs like to talk about navigating this technology in the "fog of war", which is fair, as ideally they would want to go much much slower (I earnestly believe this from most of the companies). However, I think the bigger, and more prescient issue is that the public thought they were/are getting ChatGPT-7, or Fable 6, not a more intelligent alien being that we will have to share the planet with, which will most likely lead to our disempowerment. Even now, when I read about legislation aiming to ban superintelligence (a proposal i don't fundamentally agree with, but am sympathetic to), I read replies saying things along the lines of "you can't ban open-weights!" or "it's software! you can't ban software!", and I can only sigh at the deep wilful ignorance of these people.

In a way, I believe that the marketing of AI has done great deals in making this possible for the AI industry and the reckless race towards superintelligence, as a lot of people, even those who are educated, see AI as slop or stochastic parrots, and that the bubble is going to burst (which it probably will, I still think). Early ChatGPT models were so bad, especially image generation, that people refuse to acknowledge how capable they currently are, which infuriates me, because it is extremely counterproductive from people who are unusually vocal about politics. This is rapidly changing though, and I think the median person will be completely freaked out by the SF transhumanist and rationalist ideologies.

![](/images/posts/people-just-don-t-get-it/pasted-1254df.png)

This is a good thing, while some people will turn to nihilism or defeatism in the face of imminent RSI and superintelligence, I believe it will become a salient political issue, and I believe we can put superintelligence back in the box still (after all, it is still a bunch of numbers, which can be deleted). Whether we will is a different question, but I expect the overton window to shift extremely rapidly on this issue, to the point where a Covid like response might happen (aka an AI pause/shutdown/nationalisation of frontier labs and global agreement) if politicians actually internalise what is coming, which seems to be happening.
